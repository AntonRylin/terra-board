What is it?

An infinite whiteboard for notes, plans, images and PDFs. A desktop app, not a website.

Is it free?

Yes. Free and open source under the MIT licence. No trial, no board limit, no account.

Is it safe?

The code is public. Boards are saved in a folder on your PC. There are no analytics or trackers of our own in the app.

How do I get it?

Download the installer, run it, open Terra Board from the Start menu. It updates itself.

What it does

Everything below works on your own computer. The things that use the internet are optional and marked.

An infinite canvas

Pan and zoom freely. Add text, sticky notes, shapes, frames, arrows (straight, curved or elbow), a freehand pen and a highlighter, checklists and tables with typed columns. Undo, search with Ctrl+K, and a board history (Ctrl+H) that brings back deleted things for 30 days.

Images, video and PDFs

Paste or drop images and MP4/WebM videos; crop and rotate them. Read PDFs, reorder, rotate and merge pages, and convert to images or text. Copy text out of screenshots and scanned pages with Windows' own text recognition, on your PC.

Cloud sync and sharing optional

Off until you turn it on. Keep your library the same on several computers, share a board or folder with other people, and see their cursors live. Edits made offline wait and upload when you're back online.

Share over Wi-Fi optional

People on the same network open your board in their browser, served straight from your PC. You approve each guest and choose view or edit. Requests from the public internet are refused.

AI access over MCP off by default

If you want, Claude or Codex on the same computer can read and edit your boards. It listens only on this computer and needs a private token. Terra itself never contacts an AI provider.

Your own server for companies

Run your own sync server (Supabase) and point the app at it. IT can preset or lock the server for every PC. Boards that stay local never need a server.

Your files, in a folder you can find

Boards live in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Terra Board\Library. Updating or uninstalling the app never deletes them. Export a board as a .canvas file with its original media, or as PNG, JPG or PDF.

Extras like background removal and old Office file conversion are separate downloads in Settings → Extensions, so the app stays small.

AI only when you switch it on

The connection for Claude and Codex lives in Settings → AI connections and starts switched off. AI apps can send what they read from your boards to their own model provider, so turn it on only for the boards and tools you trust.

What it doesn't do (yet)

Better you hear it here than after installing.

  • Windows only. 64-bit Windows. No Mac, Linux, phone or browser version.
  • The installer isn't code-signed, so Windows shows a warning the first time.
  • No email sign-in for sync yet. Each computer joins as its own device. If you lose every paired computer, the cloud copy can't be reached.
  • It can't open Miro or Microsoft Whiteboard files. Anything you can save as an image or PDF can be dropped onto a board.
  • Screen readers: the canvas isn't fully navigable with one yet.
  • One developer. Questions are answered by the person who wrote the code, which is good, but not around the clock.

What it connects to

“Works offline” means you can use everything on your boards without internet. When it is online, this is what the app talks to:

  • GitHub, to check for and download new versions.
  • The page behind a link you paste, to build its preview card. YouTube links play in YouTube's player on the board.
  • Websites you open in a browser frame on a board.
  • GitHub or LibreOffice's website, only when you install an extension. Each download is checked against a fingerprint built into the app.
  • The sync server, only after you turn on cloud sync or sharing.

Install

  1. Download Terra-Board-Setup-x64.exe.
  2. Run it. If Windows says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info → Run anyway.
  3. Open Terra Board from the Start menu or desktop.

New versions download in the background; click Restart to update when the app asks, or they install the next time you quit. Prefer to build it yourself? The README has the steps.

Who makes it

Terra Board is built by Anton, one developer in Austria, as the whiteboard he wanted to use himself: fast, offline, and his own files. It's published under the name Social Artworks. The app is free and open source.
